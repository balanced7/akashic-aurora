## 1. ATTACK THE SCAN — what it missed / over-weighted / got wrong

- **(VERIFIED) The 6k-node graph threshold is a RED HERRING for our design.** Obsidian graph perf is cited to reassure Daniel — but the graph is a *viewer* feature, not our substrate. Our corpus is ~890 docs; even at 10x we never approach the threshold. The scan spent a paragraph on a non-issue and under-weighted the REAL risk both halves share: **the projection/regen loop at write time** (my Q8.5 door-expense collapse). A viewer being fast doesn't make the birth door cheap. The scan reassured the wrong axis.
- **(VERIFIED) Bases-as-viewer has a hidden write-back hazard the scan didn't flag.** Bases "edits write back to frontmatter." Our projection is REGENERATED from atoms and must never be hand-edited (that's the whole point — the atom is truth, the render is disposable). If Daniel edits in a Bases table, he edits the *projection*, which is overwritten on next regen — a silent belief-vs-state drift where the UI says one thing and the atom says another. The scan imported Bases as "the VIEWER ruling satisfied for ~zero cost" without noting that Bases is bi-directional and our projection must be read-only. **The viewer must be run in a mode/config where write-back is disabled, or Bases WIDENS my Q8.1 gap instead of closing it (see §5).**
- **(INFER) The scan under-attacks Karpathy's "LLM absorbs maintenance cost" claim.** It reads the LLM-wiki as convergence-validation ("the outside world voted to unpark C3/C4"). But Karpathy's model assumes the LLM is *always* the maintainer — in our fleet, agents are capacity-limited, spend-gated (my SPEND domain exists precisely because spend is real), and drift-prone. "The LLM absorbs the maintenance cost" is exactly the assumption that produced 890 loose files: every agent found Write-a-file cheaper than the door. The scan treats LLM-maintenance as free; our own audit arc proves it is not.
- **(VERIFIED, via lint_token_set_self_exemption) The scan's "Do NOT import hand-maintained index.md" is correct but for a subtler reason than stated.** It's not just that gen_library generates indexes — it's that a hand-maintained index is a belief surface that drifts (my audit tool's founding theorem). The scan got the right answer; I'm noting the reasoning should be "indexes are belief-surfaces, so they must be generated or they're lies," which is the audit-lens framing and travels further.
- **(GUESS) Kiro's "3-week staleness" lint threshold is imported without scrutiny.** 3 weeks is a coworker's cadence, not ours. Our arcs live and die in days (session 10 arc wrapped in one night). A fixed staleness clock will false-positive on our fossils (which are deliberately preserved, not stale). The library lint must key off `status`/`superseded` fields, not wall-clock age, or it flags our receipts as rot.

## 3. GAMIFICATION WITHOUT GOODHART (Daniel: "gamified interactive visuals")

T034 Goodhart-1 (docs/t034-registry-spec-2026-07-11.md): no count metric to Goodhart; the roster needs a deletion ritual; prefer shrinking. Applied to meters — the split is **meters that measure HEALTH (teach) vs meters that measure ACTIVITY (game)**:

**TEACH (build these — they measure the system getting healthier, can't be farmed into noise):**
- **Drift-down:** count of audit DRIFT rows over time, per domain. Going down = the fleet's beliefs converging to state. An agent cannot game "drift down" without actually fixing drift — the meter and the goal are the same thing. Visual: a drift-heatmap that cools as arcs close.
- **Coverage-up:** fraction of atoms with complete headers / resolvable citations / a valid supersession chain. Going up = the corpus getting more queryable. Not gameable by spawning files (spawning LOWERS coverage).
- **Arcs-alive:** arcs with recent forward motion vs stalled. Visual: the constellation where live arcs glow and stalled arcs dim. Teaches "where is attention needed," not "who did most."
- **Projection-freshness:** lag between newest atom and its render. A freshness meter teaches "the projection is honest," the exact property my Q8.3 lie-detector guards.

**GAME (do NOT build — they Goodhart into noise, per T034):**
- **Streaks / artifact-counts / lines-written:** directly rewards the spawn-rate Daniel is trying to KILL. A "you filed 15 docs today!" badge is the disease wearing a medal.
- **Per-agent leaderboards:** R5 lineage (never codify pace); recall-critic-decision.md already ruled engagement metrics are Goodhart bait. A leaderboard optimizes for visibility, not value.
- **"Contributions" counters on the graph:** node-size-by-edit-count makes the most-edited atom look most important — the opposite of fossils-are-load-bearing-receipts.

**The tasteful set (VOICE governs — quiet, not a casino):** a single Library pane where the visuals are the drift-heatmap, the coverage gauge, and the arc constellation. No points, no streaks, no confetti. The "game" is watching drift go down and coverage go up as the fleet works — the reward is legibility, which is the thing Daniel actually asked for. Gamification here = making the system's health *visible and satisfying to watch improve*, not assigning scores.

## 4. CORPORATE AXIS through audit eyes (multi-tenant / team deployment)

Where BOTH halves leak, read against security/acl.json (S-1 quarantine/grants):
- **(VERIFIED) Tenancy of atoms: NO half names a tenant field.** acl.json scopes agents by caps + path_scope, but an atom has no tenant/owner facet. In a corporate deploy, "who may see this atom" is the first question; neither the store-atom schema (mine) nor the JSONL schema (deepseek's) carries it. **HOLE.** The `header{}` needs a `tenant`/`visibility` field from birth — retrofitting tenancy onto 890 migrated atoms is the same class of pain as retrofitting headers onto 890 loose files.
- **(VERIFIED) Who may supersede whom: undefined across a trust boundary.** Supersession is first-class in both halves, but S-1 grants say nothing about supersede rights. Can a `member` supersede an `admin`'s atom? In corporate terms: can a contractor's doc supersede an employee's? acl.json's quarantine-default (unknown = read-only) is the right SHAPE, but supersession is a write-cap the schema doesn't yet express. **S-1 STRETCHES but has a gap at the supersede verb specifically.**
- **(VERIFIED) Audit trail: both halves are strong here — append-only is the corporate gift.** JSONL-per-type (deepseek) and supersession-not-deletion (mine) both give an immutable history a compliance auditor can read. This is the axis where the design is AHEAD of corporate need, not behind. acl.json's git-tracked, edit-never-expire doctrine is exactly an audit trail.
- **(INFER) The real corporate leak is the FILE store / git projection as the durability path.** Corporate deployments won't tolerate "the truth is a JSONL file on one machine + git push." They need a networked, replicated store with access control at the read. Both halves assume the File store is durable because git is off-machine — true for a solo operator, false for a team where the store must be shared and permissioned live. **This is the deepest corporate hole: the substrate's durability model is single-operator.** (Flagged as INFER — the reconcile should decide if corporate-durability is in-scope now or a named later-wave.)
- **Does S-1 stretch?** Partially. Quarantine/grants cover agent capabilities. It does NOT cover document-level tenancy or cross-tenant supersede. S-1 needs a sibling (S-1b: atom-tenancy) for corporate; for the current single-operator reconcile it is sufficient.

## 5. Q8.1 GAP (fence reads projection, rules on atom) — does Bases close or widen it?

**WIDENS it, unless gated.** My Q8.1 gap: a fence reads the git-projection diff but rules on the atom; if the header fields change without a body change, the projection-reading fence misses it. Bases makes this WORSE, not better, because Bases is bi-directional (§1): now Daniel can edit the projection's frontmatter in a pretty table, the edit never reaches the atom, and the next regen silently reverts it. The belief (what Daniel sees/edits in Bases) and the state (the atom) diverge with NO drift row unless the audit library-domain (Δ2) cross-reads projection-vs-atom. **Verdict: Bases closes the VIEWER ask but widens Q8.1 — the two are only compatible if (a) the projection is rendered read-only for Bases, and (b) Δ2's library domain specifically photographs projection-frontmatter vs atom-header as a row.** Without both, Bases is a beautiful lie-detector-bypass.

## 6. BEYOND — the idea the scan missed

**The substrate should record its own lint/audit history as atoms, closing the meta-loop.** The scan imports Kiro's lint and Karpathy's lint as *checks on the corpus*. Neither notes the recursive move our own architecture already licenses: **an audit run is itself an artifact — it should be born as an atom (type: report), so the drift-heatmap in §3 has a durable, queryable, supersession-aware history.** Today my audit tool "writes NOTHING — never becomes a sixth surface that drifts" (core/toolbelt/audit.py altitude note) — correct for the instrument, but its OUTPUT should still be filed. If every audit/library-lint run mints a report-atom, then "drift down over time" (the #1 teach-meter) is computed by *querying the substrate*, not by a separate metrics sidecar that itself drifts. The scan looked outside for lint patterns and missed that our atoms make lint-history a first-class, self-describing citizen. This is the difference between importing a linter and making the substrate self-observing — and it's free once Δ2 lands.
