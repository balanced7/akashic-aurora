1. STATIC ANALYSIS / LINT AT COMMIT-TIME (GitHub CodeQL/Code Scanning; Semgrep; Coverity). MECHANISM: trigger = an ACTION (a commit/diff), NOT a query -- the closest structural match to us. They ship per-rule CONFIDENCE/SEVERITY tiers and only auto-fire the high-precision subset inline; low-confidence rules are off-by-default or advisory-only, precisely to protect trust. The shipped engineering is a PRECISION FLOOR per rule: a rule whose measured FP rate is too high does not get to interrupt. EVIDENCE: INFER -- Coverity and CodeQL market low-FP as their core claim; Semgrep documents per-rule confidence. No hard public number I can stand behind. WHERE IT BREAKS (mandatory): their corpus is CODE with a formal semantics -- a rule can be PROVEN to match a pattern, and the FP rate is measurable because there is a ground truth (does the pattern exist / is it a real bug). Our corpus is agent PROSE with no ground truth under it; we cannot compute a rule's FP rate the way they compute a checker's. THE TRANSFER: the per-item precision floor + tiered interruption (high-confidence interrupts, low-confidence is silent) -- NOT their verification.

2. SPAM FILTERING (Gmail). MECHANISM: the base-rate problem, solved at planetary scale -- the vast majority of incoming is junk and the FP cost (real mail in spam) is catastrophic, so the system is built around NOT false-positiving. Two shipped mechanisms transfer: (a) precision-by-default -- when uncertain, deliver to inbox (the low-regret default), only act on high-confidence spam; (b) per-user implicit feedback (move-to-spam / not-spam) aggregated as a prior. EVIDENCE: INFER -- Gmail cites spam FP rates around 0.05% and ~99.9% spam catch in older Google blog posts; I am NOT sure of these numbers and they are marketing-adjacent. WHERE IT BREAKS: they have a BALANCED, massive implicit-feedback stream (billions of explicit not-spam actions) giving a per-user ground signal; we have sparse implicit feedback and CANNOT tell "used it" from "was relevant" -- the exact asymmetry you named. Also spam is a stationary class (junk stays junk); our items have wildly different validity lifetimes (your DECAY axis), which spam does not face. THE TRANSFER: the asymmetric-cost default (when unsure, DON'T inject -- a silent miss is cheaper than a trust-eroding false positive) and the idea that FP cost, not catch rate, sets the operating point.

3. CLINICAL DECISION SUPPORT / EHR ALERTS (Epic/Cerner drug-interaction + order-set alerts). MECHANISM: trigger = an ACTION at the moment of work (a physician entering an order), unrequested, tiny budget, FP cost = alert fatigue (the canonical finding: clinicians override the large majority of alerts, and the override itself becomes the hazard). Shipped mitigations: TIERED alerts (only a small interruptive tier; most alerts passive), plus ALERT-FATIGUE measurement (override rate as the health metric). EVIDENCE: INFER -- the alert-fatigue literature (Bates, McCoy, Staggers) reports override rates commonly cited ~50-95% for drug-interaction alerts; this is a real, well-studied number but I am reciting a range, not a verified figure. WHERE IT BREAKS: they have a structured, finite drug-interaction KNOWLEDGE BASE (authoritative, curated by pharmacists) -- the items are not agent prose, and relevance is determined by a formal drug-pair table, not similarity. We have no such table. THE TRANSFER, and it is the sharpest for us: OVERRIDE RATE as the primary health metric of an interruptive system (you already named this in your override-rate slices) and TIERED interruption -- most alerts passive, only the highest-stakes interrupt. Their decades of evidence: an interruptive advisor with a high FP rate trains the user to ignore it, which is EXACTLY Daniel's "recall keeps surfacing invalid items."

4. RECOMMENDER / RANKING WITH EXPLICIT PRECISION-AT-K + GUARDRAILS (Netflix; LinkedIn feed). MECHANISM: the no-explicit-query ranking problem (the "because you watched X" home row is closer to our no-query trigger than search is). Shipped: precision@k offline metrics plus INTERLEAVING for online eval (they cannot A/B naively either), and a hard diversity/freshness guardrail so one over-weighted cluster does not dominate the top-k. EVIDENCE: INFER -- Netflix's interleaving work (their research blog) and the standard precision@k practice; no verified number. WHERE IT BREAKS, and this is the deep one: they have a CLICKSTREAM -- a dense, continuous implicit-feedback signal (plays, watch-time) that grounds relevance. We do not have a clickstream; our feedback is too sparse to train a ranker. Without it their whole learning-to-rank stack is unavailable to us. THE TRANSFER: the diversity/freshness GUARDRAIL over top-k (a single rule, no ML) and interleaving as the offline-impossible eval method (deepseek's propensity point) -- NOT the learned ranker.

5. INTRUSION / ANOMALY DETECTION (the base-rate fallacy, Axelsson 1999). MECHANISM: not a system but the governing theory of our exact numbers -- at a 0.5% base rate, even a 99%-accurate detector floods with false positives; Axelsson showed IDS must reach near-impossible FP rates to be usable at low base rates. EVIDENCE: INFER -- Axelsson's base-rate fallacy paper is real and canonical; the implication is arithmetic, not a measurement. WHERE IT BREAKS: it is a constraint, not a solution -- it tells us the operating point is brutally hard, not how to reach it. THE TRANSFER: at our base rate, the ONLY viable operating point is a very high per-item precision threshold, which means injecting NOTHING most of the time -- silent-when-starved is not a fallback, it is the mathematically forced default. This reframes "precision 0.30" (my label count): at 0.5% base rate, the fix is not a better ranker alone, it is a far more conservative injection policy.

NET SYNTHESIS FOR THE INTEGRATION: three fields converge on the same three mechanisms, all buildable WITHOUT the data we lack -- (i) a per-item PRECISION FLOOR / tiered interruption (static analysis + CDS): only high-confidence items interrupt, the rest are passive/silent; (ii) asymmetric-cost default (spam): when unsure, DON'T inject; (iii) OVERRIDE RATE as the health metric (CDS, already your slice). The two that need data we do NOT have -- learned-to-rank (recommenders) and per-user feedback priors (spam) -- are the traps; name them so we do not chase them. The base-rate math (IDS) says the dominant lever is INJECTION POLICY (inject less), which is cheaper and more reversible than any ranker or cell. My strongest claim: at 0.5% base rate and 0.30 measured precision, the first build is not ranking and not cells -- it is a precision-floor gate that makes recall SILENT by default and fires only the high-confidence tail.
