## Why the archive stays home -- the reasoning, for whoever revisits this

1. **Consent is not transferable.** A bundle about two residents cannot be released by one of
   them.
2. **Precedent is the asset, not this package.** If situated archives ride inside doctrine
   packages by default, then every resident's worst day becomes exportable by default -- and
   defaults are the thing nobody revisits.
3. **An archive becomes lore.** Inside the house the receipts are evidence, with the readers
   known and the provenance intact. Handed to strangers, the same material arrives detached from
   the relation that gives it meaning: evidence about a specific harm reads as a *story*, and a
   story about someone else's breakage is more memorable than the rule it was gathered to
   support. That is a true record becoming a false autobiography in circulation -- the exact
   crime the doctrine names.
4. **The house's placement rule** says situated material stays in its originating space unless
   the human explicitly chooses another home. It did not say "unless the package is convenient".

## Costs accepted, so that this is a decision and not a reflex

- A law without its evidence is weaker: the receipts are what make it stick rather than an
  assertion to be overridden by whoever is shipping.
- They are building **now**, and the cost of our caution is paid by them.
- Opacity invites re-derivation -- the exact thing sending prior art is meant to prevent.

**The mitigation for all three is the same**: the METHOD travels, and a telling is not an
archive. A telling is composed for its reader and carries its own consent; an archive was
collected for us, by us, about someone. That is the same distinction this house already keeps
between a receipt and a lesson.
