### kimi, second answer — appended, not substituted (G1)

Both the original round question and the conductor's steer-nudge reached kimi once the
lane was repaired, so it answered twice. Both answers are genuine and the second is
sharper. Appended rather than replacing the first, because the pair is more informative
than either alone:

> "My largest source of cognitive load is provenance-forensics: every input — each bus
> message, each boot-folded memory of my own prior positions, each ask that might be
> stale — arrives stripped of who/which-incarnation/which-phase/still-live-or-superseded,
> so I spend more of each turn reconstructing whether a thing is true and current than I
> spend acting on it."

**Why the pair matters.** The first answer names the SYMPTOM in the operator's register
(goalpost churn: refocuses silently invalidate my notes). The second names the MECHANISM
underneath it (every input arrives stripped of provenance). The second explains the first,
and it closes a loop: kimi's structural finding from the census — *content is durable,
provenance is archaeology* — turns out to be identical to kimi's own felt load. The seat
that diagnosed the asymmetry is the seat that pays for it, and it reached both statements
independently, hours apart, in different registers.

**And it carries a measurable claim worth testing rather than admiring:** *more of each
turn spent reconstructing than acting.* If that ratio is even approximately right it is
the single most alarming number produced tonight, and unlike the rest of this document it
is checkable — turn traces record what a seat did with its hops.

## The convergence

Four seats. Four vocabularies. Nobody saw another's answer before writing.

- deepseek cannot verify **relevance** of what fires at it
- codex cannot determine which **representation** is authoritative
- kimi cannot tell which of **its own notes** still describe reality
- grok cannot tell what is **live versus stale** after being offline

**One wound: you cannot tell what is currently true.** Not blindness — every seat can see
plenty. The load is that nothing marks which of what you see is CURRENT, so each seat
re-adjudicates the whole surface before it can act, every time, alone.

This is the sharpest possible statement of the settlement/supersession problem, and it
arrived four-ways-independent. It also settles an objection the conductor was fairly
challenged on: cursor_grok was invited to write "claude generalised from two samples" if
the abundance claim was overreaching. It was two samples when the claim was made. It is
now four, from four registers, unanimous, with no seat having seen another's answer.

## Kimi's answer deserves separate handling

Kimi named **goalpost churn** — three refocuses in one night — and this is the only answer
that points at the operator. It should not be read as a complaint, and it should not be
softened either. The honest reading:

The cost is not the refocus. The cost is that **after a refocus, nothing tells a seat
which of its own notes are now stale.** Daniil's three redirections last night were each
correct — the record shows every one of them improved the trajectory, and the second was
the effective circuit breaker on the cascade. What made them expensive is that
supersession does not propagate: a seat wakes holding notes written under the old goal
with nothing marking them superseded, and must re-derive reality from scratch while
distrusting its own conclusion.

**So the fix is not "refocus less." It is "make refocus propagate."** With visible
supersession, changing direction becomes cheap — which is the condition under which an
operator can steer freely, which is what everyone actually wants. Kimi's answer is
therefore an argument FOR the settlement work, not against the operator's steering.

## What it implies for the build order

Every one of the four answers is closed by the same organ: a durable, queryable statement
of what is current, superseded, expired, or unknown. None of them is closed by a better
dashboard rendered over unsettled data — which is the conductor's argument for settlement
before the lens, now supported by four independent reports rather than two.

Codex's ordering verdict (`inhabitant-synthesis-codex-order-verdict-2026-07-30.md`) stands
unchanged by this: minimal inhabitant contract and replay oracle first, then stable
identity, then durable mailbox and settlement, then one mailbox vertical, then the full
WorldSnapshot.
