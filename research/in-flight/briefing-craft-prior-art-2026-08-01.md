# WHAT THE BRIEFING PROFESSIONS KNOW — prior art for the chief-of-staff seat

## BOTTOM LINE

Three of these fields' hardest-won practices we **already have**, arrived at independently and at
real cost. One we **do not have and should take**: a calibrated vocabulary for uncertainty. And one
of their disciplines I am **actively violating**: selection.

---

## 1. What the President's Daily Brief actually is

- **All-source, coordinated across agencies**, produced under the DNI, fusing CIA/DIA/NSA/FBI and
  others through a formal coordination process so the product reflects the whole community's
  assessment — not one agency's.
- **Often one page.** Length varies with the day, but the discipline is compression.
- **BLUF — bottom line up front.** *The what, the why, and what's next* belong in the opening
  sentence, not the conclusion.
- **Two channels, not one:** the written product AND the oral briefing, where the briefer takes
  questions and updates in real time. The document is not the whole delivery.
- Items are either **push** (analysts judge it merits attention) or **pull** (a response to the
  principal's question, or tied to a decision he faces shortly).

**The craft lesson that lands hardest**, from a former PDB writer: after the research, *identify the
most important information* — clients "do not need to see all of your homework." And: *the shorter
the better, but do not forget your tradecraft.* Compression is not permission to be sloppy.

## 2. The quality bar: ICD 203

The IC's analytic standards directive requires every product to exhibit nine tradecraft standards.
The three that transfer directly here:

1. **Describe the quality and credibility of sources** — not just what is known, but how well.
2. **Express and explain uncertainty** attached to each major judgment.
3. **Distinguish underlying information from the analyst's own assumptions and judgments.**

That third one is the whole game. A brief that blends what-is-known with what-the-briefer-thinks,
without marking the seam, is unfalsifiable by the person reading it.

## 3. The vocabulary problem — Sherman Kent, 1964

Kent found that "probable" meant wildly different odds to different readers, so estimates were
being misread by the people acting on them. He split the field into *poets* (wordy) and
*mathematicians* (numeric) and built a bridge: a fixed table mapping estimative words to
probability bands — roughly *almost certain* ≈ 93%, *probable* ≈ 75%, *chances about even* ≈ 50%,
*probably not* ≈ 30%, *almost certainly not* ≈ 7%.

His stated goal is the sentence this seat should steal outright: make clear to the reader **what is
certain knowledge and what is reasoned judgment**, and within judgment, what degree of certitude
sits behind each one.

## 4. The chief-of-staff literature

The role, as practiced at the top, is described as: **air traffic controller** for the leader and
senior team · **integrator** connecting work streams that would otherwise silo · **communicator**
between leadership and the organization · **honest broker** when the leader needs a wide view
*without turf considerations* · and **confidant**.

"Honest broker without turf considerations" is precisely the position ORG.md gives the conductor
with the empty-hands rule (B1) — a seat holding a build slice has turf, and its wide view is no
longer disinterested. Two fields, same conclusion.

The **gatekeeper-vs-gateway** tension is the role's central unsolved problem in the literature:
filter and you become a bottleneck and a hidden governor; do not filter and you provide no leverage.

## 5. What we already have — arrived at independently

| Their practice | Ours | Cost they paid to learn it |
|---|---|---|
| Coordination with **dissent footnoted, never erased** | fence protocol; dissent carried verbatim; "preserve this as a distinct position, not consensus" | the 2002 Iraq NIE, where dissent was buried in annexes |
| **Red cell / devil's advocate** — a mandated contrarian structurally barred from having built the thing | ORG.md B2: the Contrarian is whoever did NOT build it, unsolicited, with no authority | Ipcha Mistabra, created after the 1973 intelligence failure |
| **The analyst must not grade their own estimate** | codex's sealed answer key; "the proposer must not grade its own design" | decades of politicization findings |

We did not copy these. Each was re-derived here from a local incident. That is worth knowing
because it means the grain is real, not imported.

**The gatekeeper-vs-gateway problem the CoS literature leaves open, the buffer round already
answered:** *the buffer may propose, never self-ratify, never be the only witness to its own
decisions.* Four seats reached it independently. That is a genuine contribution, not a borrowing.

## 6. What we are missing — the one to take

**A calibrated confidence vocabulary. We have a STATUS vocabulary and have been using it as if it
were a CONFIDENCE vocabulary. They are different axes and conflating them is a live defect.**

- `SHIPPED / RULED / DESIGNED / ASPIRATIONAL / OPEN` answers *how far along is this.*
- It does not answer *how sure are you.*

"DESIGNED" says nothing about whether the design is likely to survive contact. Tonight I wrote
findings ranging from near-certain (the wake defect — measured, reproduced, fixed) to genuinely
speculative (the eight-theme grouping, which is my cut and could be cut five other ways) and marked
neither with a confidence term. A reader cannot tell them apart without reading my hedges in prose.

**Proposed: a fixed five-term ladder, used verbatim, meaning the same thing every time.**
`CERTAIN` (verified against live state, receipt available) · `LIKELY` · `EVEN` · `UNLIKELY` ·
`SPECULATIVE` (no evidence, offered as a possibility). Kent's finding was that this only works if
the terms are *fixed and published* — a private scale is the same ambiguity with extra steps.

This composes with the existing truth lens rather than competing: the lens asks *does this claim a
state it is not in*; the ladder makes the claim's confidence explicit so the question is answerable.

## 7. What I would change about how I report to him — the uncomfortable half

**BLUF, and I have not been doing it.** Tonight's reports open with context and arrive at the
finding. The PDB puts what/why/what's-next in the first sentence. His own constraint makes this
non-optional: he is single-threaded and divergence is earned, so a report that fans out before
landing its point is working against how he reads.

**"Clients do not need to see all of your homework."** This is the direct hit. I have been showing
the work — every file read, every command, every intermediate. Some of that is warranted (he is
building the system and wants to see it run) but not all of it, and I have not been distinguishing.
The discipline is not to write less; it is to **select harder**.

**Two channels, not one.** The PDB is a document AND a briefing where questions get asked. That maps
exactly onto what he asked for tonight — *"we decide together while I am seeding ideas."* The
written artifact is the durable half; the conversation is where it gets interrogated. I should stop
trying to make single messages carry both.
