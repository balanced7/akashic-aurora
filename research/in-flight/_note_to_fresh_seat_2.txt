1) YOUR CURATOR-FLIP-RATE IDEA IS THE BEST THING IN THE DESIGN, AND IT HAS ONE TRAP.

Generalising flip-rate from the evidence gatherer to the CURATION tier is a real extension
of L2 -- I never applied it there. Keep it.

The trap: when two curators over the same material disagree, that has THREE causes, not
two. You named ambiguity and curation artifact. The third is that THEY DID NOT ACTUALLY
RECEIVE THE SAME INPUTS -- and I shipped exactly that bug tonight (T216, 54bc84c): --with
was accepted on the fan path and SILENTLY dropped, so five helpers got zero files while
every answer came back well-formed. It survived a night of heavy use because my big runs
inlined files by hand.

So before you read any curator diff as signal, assert input identity: hash the evidence
blob each curator received and refuse to compute a flip rate unless the hashes match.
Otherwise you will measure INPUT divergence and report it as curation noise -- which is
the same defect one tier up from where I found it.

2) PER-TERM vs PER-HAT, since you were weighing it.

Per-term for the dossier (a word is the decidable unit -- you can act on "open"), but note
that ONLY per-term overlap makes your comparison round computable: two curators wearing
different hats on different terms cannot be diffed at all, while two curators on the SAME
term produce a diff whose disagreements are located. So the overlap you want is per-term,
2 curators per term, hats varied WITHIN each pair rather than across pairs. That gives you
dissent that points at a word instead of at a methodology.

Cheap version if budget bites: overlap only the terms where layer 1 itself disagreed. The
consensus terms do not need two curators, and layer-1 disagreement is free to compute.
