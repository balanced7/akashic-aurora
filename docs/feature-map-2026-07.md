## THE NAMING SCHEME — the Two-Register Law

Every named thing carries two registers:
- **PLAIN** — function-first, stranger-guessable. The API. (`capture`, `parse-gate`, `drain-decide`
  — our best verbs already obey.)
- **SOUL** — the charter name: family, callsign, lore. The depth. Lives in the registry's
  family/flavor fields, the docs, the culture.

**Rule 1 — First Glance (the Stranger Test).** The soul name may FRONT only when the borrowed
mechanic is famous and exact enough to TEACH the function by itself: `scry`, `mulligan`, `kata`,
`medivac`, `flashback` self-explain to most players. Otherwise plain fronts; soul enriches.
**Rule 2 — No Lies.** A name promises no more than the thing does (LEXICON law, check_boundaries
lineage). Corollary: no graduated regalia on an untested thing — a larva cannot wear N7.
**Rule 3 — One Franchise Per Layer.** Halo = places/castes/organs · MTG = interaction law ·
SC2 = lifecycle/culture · ME = trust/continuity. Cross-layer borrowing is soup; veto on sight.
**Rule 4 — Pattern Consistency** (semantic_naming_pattern_discovery lineage): verbs are
imperative-lowercase-hyphenated (`drain-decide`); families are PLURAL CASTES (`SENTINELS`);
places are THE-Proper (`the Domain`); personas are singular proper (`Abathur`, `Catalog`);
stages are bare nouns (`larva`, `chrysalis`).
**Rule 5 — Depth Pays Rent.** Every soul name carries a one-line "why the metaphor is
load-bearing." Can't write the line → it's decoration → drop it.
