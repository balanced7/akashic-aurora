# Fact sourcing: the provenance ladder (draft rules)

Status: DRAFT rules for every fact that appears in our videos: now-playing cards, deep dives, instrument histories and film appearances. Proposed 2026-10-07 by Vandor from Daniel's principle; pending Daniel's ratification. Arc: media-suite.

## Daniel's principle (verbatim)

"on provenance we can make guides and rules for sourcing, something the author or engineer said themselves can probably be quoted because its literally directly what was said. you can think of other strategies as well"

## The ladder (strongest → weakest)

1. **First-person and traceable.** The artist, engineer, producer or guitar tech in their own words, traced to the original artifact:
   - a filmed interview, with a timestamp;
   - their own book, liner notes or post;
   - a documentary where they speak;
   - a rig-rundown video where they or their tech show the actual gear.
2. **Primary records.**
   - album credits and liner notes, session logs, official discographies;
   - the maker's records (serial numbers);
   - auction catalogs for famous instruments;
   - a film's own credits, for "what movie was it in".
3. **Reputable secondary.** Music journalism with editorial standards, and serious biographies.
4. **Finding aids.** Wikipedia, Wikidata, MusicBrainz, Discogs, Equipboard, IMDb soundtrack lists. Use them to FIND the root source; never cite them as the root.
5. **Unsourced lore.** Quote sites, listicles, forum lore, uncited social posts. Never on screen as fact; only as a clearly labeled legend.

## Rules

1. **Attribution framing.** Put claims on screen as what a source says: "In a 1987 interview, X said…", "The liner notes credit…". A framed claim is true by construction, and disagreement between sources becomes content: "X remembers A; his drummer remembers B."
2. **A quote proves what was said, not that it is true.** A first-person quote is the strongest evidence of the words, so present it as their words, not as bare fact. Artists retell legends.
3. **Trace every quote to the original:** outlet, date, and a timestamp for video. Quote-aggregator sites are not sources; misattributed quotes are everywhere.
4. **Two independent sources for any bare (unattributed) assertion.** Independent means they don't cite each other: two articles quoting Wikipedia count as one source.
5. **Follow the citation chain to the root.** Record the root, not the aggregator.
6. **Legends are labeled as legends.** Myth vs. record is its own strong format: "The legend says… the session log says…".
7. **Instrument provenance has its own evidence:**
   - serial numbers and maker records;
   - auction catalogs;
   - dated photos (wear, cracks, stickers and mods are how guitar historians match one instrument across photos);
   - first-person rig walkthroughs.
8. **Perishable vs. stable.** Birthdays and release dates are stable; "still owns" and "currently touring" go stale. Mark perishable facts and re-check them before reuse.
9. **Every fact is stored with its provenance:**
   - the claim and its rung;
   - the root source (URL, outlet, date, timestamp);
   - the quote text, if it is a quote;
   - who verified it, and when.
10. **The model retrieves and drafts; it never supplies facts from memory.** The citation is the retrieved source.
11. **Sources on screen.** A small outlet + year tag on each card, a brand feature that specialists respect.
12. **A corrections ledger.** When a viewer corrects us: verify, fix the fact store, and consider a correction video. A correction handled well is both credibility and content.

## Rights note

Quoting a person's words as attributed text, as part of commentary, is the classic fair-use case. Playing the interview clip itself is a separate rights question, because the footage is copyrighted. Default: quote the words and cite them; clip only when rights allow. [This is not legal advice.]
