HDR check

What this browser and screen report, and still test patches to check HDR and wide colour by eye.

Nothing on this page flashes or moves. The brighter-than-white patches are small on purpose: the OLED dims large bright areas.

Checking...

If the brighter squares all look the same as white

Then Windows is leaving Chrome no room above white. On 2026-10-24 the machine was set to an SDR white of 480 nits (the "SDR content brightness" slider at maximum) while the panel reports a peak of about 400 nits, so Chrome gets only 6% above white and 2×, 4× and 8× all clip to plain white. This page cannot read or change those settings. To give it room (your call):

  1. Windows Settings, System, Display, HDR: set SDR content brightness to about 25–30%.
  2. On the monitor's menu: switch to the Peak 1000 HDR mode.
  3. Run the Windows HDR Calibration app again in that mode, with its saturation slider in the middle.
  4. Reload this page. Expect roughly 5× above white, for small highlights only.

1. Brighter than white

If HDR works: each square to the right is clearly brighter than the white square on the left; if all of them look the same, see the box above.

2. Find your headroom

If HDR works: the squares keep getting brighter up to some step; click the last one that is still brighter than its left neighbour, and the page remembers that number for this screen.

3. Laser beams on black

1× white
(the most a normal page can do)
6× white
(HDR core)

If HDR works: the lower beams burn noticeably hotter and more vivid than the upper ones while the black around them stays pure black; that is the OLED's highlight headroom.

4. Wide colour (page colours)

If wide colour works: each tile shows a deeper, more saturated square inside it (Display-P3), sometimes with an even deeper dot in the middle (Rec.2020); a flat tile means the colour is squeezed to sRGB.

5. Wide colour, drawn the way the VJ rig draws

red · yellow · green · cyan · blue · magenta (WebGL2, Display-P3 drawing buffer)

If wide colour works: the inner squares show here as well, which means the rig's WebGL2 drawing can reach the deeper colours too.

6. Banding in dark gradients

Standard 8-bit drawing buffer (the WebGL default)

Float16 drawing buffer

rows: black to 2% grey · black to deep blue · black to white

If the float buffer helps: its top two strips fade smoothly where the 8-bit strips show visible steps, so dark fog would stop banding.

7. Near-black: lifted shadows

In Windows HDR mode: both rows are meant to be the same four very dark greys, but the lower row (encoded with plain gamma 2.2, as the rig does today) looks lighter and greyer, which is part of the haze.
