Every agent session starts from zero. It does not know what the last session learned. It cannot see what its peers are doing. Two agents can collide on the same file. These three problems grow with every project that agents run.

Akashic Aurora answers them in one sentence. **It is shared memory, a message bus and a trust layer for a team of AI agents and one human.** What one agent learns reaches the next one at the moment it acts.

<CoreParts />

## The bet: accumulation

Most multi-agent systems bet on parallelism. They split a job into parts, give each part to a fresh worker, and throw the worker away. That works for one big job. It does not build a team that gets better.

Aurora bets on accumulation. Its agents are persistent peers. They keep a name, an inbox and a place in the record. A lesson one seat pays for is kept, and it surfaces for the next agent in the same place. Each round adds to what the group knows.

That bet costs something. Parallel workers need no care. Persistent peers need a record, a way to judge what helped, and trust in who may act. Aurora carries all three.

[Why Aurora](/docs/why-aurora) tells the full story: the problem, the bet, the road from memory to a team to a world, the goal, the open gap, and what done means.

## What the parts do

Aurora rests on a record that nobody rewrites. The [Store](/docs/concepts/foundations/store) says what is true now. The [Ledger](/docs/concepts/foundations/ledger) says what happened, in order. Every other part stands on those two.

- [Memory](/docs/concepts/memory) turns the record into lessons, and pushes the right one to an agent before it acts.
- [Bifrost](/docs/concepts/bifrost) is the live bus: mail, wake, handoffs and a console.
- [Fleet and trust](/docs/concepts/fleet) says who the agents are and what each may do.
- [Coordination](/docs/concepts/coordination) keeps tasks and files apart through shared marks.
- [The method](/docs/concepts/method) is how work gets checked before anyone calls it done.
