---
title: Charters and briefs
description: A charter is a seat's standing contract; a brief is a one-off work order handed to a seat.
---

A charter says who a [seat](/docs/concepts/bifrost/seats) is and what it is responsible for. It lasts. A brief says what to do this time. It ends when the job ends. Think of a job description next to a work ticket.

<Callout type="warn" title="Old usage remains">The word "charter" still means "work order" in some older files and a few later ones.</Callout>

## Why it exists

The word "charter" once had four meanings at the same time. Some people meant a seat's standing contract. Others meant a work order. Agents could not tell which was meant. The operator ruled that "charter" means the standing contract only. A work order is a "brief".

## How it works

**Charters** live at `charters/<agent>/CHARTER.md`. Seats such as claude, kimi, deepseek and gemini have one. A charter covers who the seat is, what it owns, and its current stretch. A stretch is the one skill the seat is reaching for, one step past what it has shown. At [boot](/docs/concepts/memory/boot), Aurora looks for a "current stretch" line in the seat's charter. Today no charter has one, so boot shows that gap by name.

**Briefs** live in `research/briefs/`. A brief gives the mission, the limits, the deliverable, and which files the seat may write. The conduct laws say how a brief opens. It starts with the purpose. It carries the operator's own words, word for word where it can.

Old files keep their old names. The ruling fixes the word from then on, not the past. But some text written after the ruling still uses "charter" for a work order. One example is `charters/sa1-cap-enforcement-charter-2026-07-22.md`, which is a brief in all but name. When you write, use "brief" for a work order.

## How you use it

There is no verb for either. You read your charter. Boot reads the stretch line from it. You write a brief when you hand work to another seat. See [hand off work](/docs/how-to/hand-off-work).

## Don't confuse it with

- **An arc** — a long line of work across weeks. See [Doctrine](/docs/concepts/coordination/doctrine).
- **A post** — a standing duty in the org guide, such as Builder or Verifier. A charter is per seat. A post is per duty.
- **A resident's callsign** — the name a seat earned. See [Fleet and residents](/docs/concepts/coordination/fleet-and-residents).
