## Thesis

**Reward meaning, not activity.** If every note is equally bright, dense playing becomes a cyan wall and the visualizer applauds quantity. Daniel's music has breath, held colours, moving bass floors, suspensions and arrivals. The picture should too.

The emotional unit is the phrase: **quiet → curiosity → build → arrival → afterglow**. That arc must remain visible with every label hidden.

## The 9:16 frame

Treat the vertical frame as three connected depths, not three panels.

### Instrument — bottom 30%

The piano is the physical anchor: dark, tactile, close enough that key motion is unmistakable, with restrained reflections rather than showroom gloss.

Visual truth at the keys:
- **finger-held:** depressed key and solid warm-white/cyan core;
- **pedal-held:** returned key with a hollow coloured rim;
- **released:** one short breath, then gone;
- **velocity:** controls attack size and initial brightness, not permanent opacity;
- **CC64 lift:** pedal-held rims close in one clean horizontal release while physically held notes remain solid.

A viewer must distinguish finger, pedal and history in one glance. Never encode all three as “more glow.”

### Memory — middle 50%

Notes rise in stable pitch lanes, but as memory rather than exhaust. The newest seconds are crisp; older material becomes thin and desaturated. Finger-held notes are solid, pedal-held notes hollow, released history a hairline. Repeated notes create beads instead of stacking white bars. Common tones remain continuous while moving voices bend between lanes. Bass has more weight but less bloom.

The scheme may be Upright Roll, Harmonic Wave, Orbit, Loom or Constellations, but this state grammar stays invariant. A preset changes poetry, not truth. Darkness between notes is part of the composition.

### Meaning — top 20%

Protect a calm reading zone. Show one primary fact and at most one secondary thought:
- primary: chord or Nashville function, large enough for a phone;
- secondary: key confidence, one voice-leading phrase, **or** staff—not all three.

Names settle after the sound settles. During ambiguous pedal harmony, show two readings or an ellipsis rather than flashing labels. Across `evidence/01–06`, the eye should land **keys → musical motion → meaning**. If text or atmosphere wins first, hierarchy is wrong.

## The session breathes

**Quiet.** Idle looks alive but nearly still: a slow exposure breath, faint horizon or grain. No prompts. The first note should feel as if it woke the room.

**Curiosity.** Let Daniel establish a phrase before analysis enters. A newly explored register or voicing can open the space slightly; familiar language should feel warm, not punished as common.

**Build.** Drive intensity from phrase energy, not raw polyphony: note density, register spread, velocity envelope, bass motion, harmonic change, repetition, pedal duration and recent silence. Integrate over seconds. Camera motion, bloom, particles and label scale share one bounded attention budget; they never all peak together.

**Arrival.** Arrival is contrast. Detect it from phrase boundary, bass/root landing, tension decrease, cadence or repetition—not chord-name confidence alone. Then: (1) motion converges for 150–250 ms; (2) the voicing becomes briefly legible as a shape; (3) colour warms or opens instead of merely turning white; (4) the name appears after the sound; (5) the camera takes one tiny breath, never a swoop. Spend the largest response only once or twice a minute.

**Afterglow.** Hold the geometry softly for 1–2 seconds. This lets Daniel see what his hands did and gives the editor a natural cut.

## The one TikTok moment

A strong vertical clip is a seven-to-twelve-second musical sentence, not a feature tour: enough quiet to establish the room; a motif or held colour; one move that creates uncertainty; a pedal lift, bass change or breath that exposes it; an arrival where the note field forms one readable image; a short afterglow with no extra prose.

Its signature is **the music pulling scattered history into one shape**: common-tone threads stay fixed, moving voices curve into place, pedal ghosts release in one line, and the final harmony draws a restrained crown/polygon/loom-knot. It is beautiful because the previous seconds explain it.

Keep title-safe margins for phone chrome. Maintain a rolling buffer so “that was cool” can save the lead-in without asking Daniel to reproduce it. Recorded timing and colour must match live output.

## Reward exploration without points

Carry the jam-space loop into presentation: **play → notice → name → alter → play again**.

- **Play:** no teaching interrupts the phrase.
- **Notice:** after a breath, one replayable marker says “this changed here.”
- **Name:** then offer a chord/function or “one useful name.”
- **Alter:** ghost one nearby possibility—one note, bass, rhythm, register or pedal choice.
- **Play again:** the ghost disappears at his first answer.

Recognition is the reward. At 1 a.m., the piano might quietly notice a fingerprint: “you found that doorway again,” linked to the earlier moment. If uncertain: “we heard two possible homes—what did you hear?” No scores, mastery bars, rarity grades or red mistakes.

Three views can share one renderer:
1. **Play:** keys, memory and one settled meaning.
2. **Notice:** replay markers, optional staff/voice-leading and one concept card.
3. **Capture:** 9:16 guides and minimal text; no controls in the recorded surface.

## Feature order

**P0 truth:** note/sustain state, velocity attack, stable pitch lanes, readable label, correct crop, deterministic recording.

**P1 phrasing:** silence/phrase detector, bounded energy envelope, common-tone continuity, arrival and afterglow.

**P2 play:** replayable notice moments, one-step ghost alterations, rolling clip buffer, three views.

**P3 delight:** personal-fingerprint callbacks and rare scheme-specific arrival gestures.

Atmosphere follows P0. A beautiful lie about which notes sound is not beautiful.

## How we know

Use the same fixed MIDI performances for every candidate, including the densest pedalled passage represented in `evidence/01–06`.

**Measured:**
- 60 fps recording: p95 frame time ≤16.7 ms; live and recording produce the same state transitions.
- Note-on to visible key p95 <30 ms; CC64 changes within one rendered frame.
- Text contrast ≥4.5:1 against the composited background; bloom never crosses the protected meaning zone.
- Finger-held and pedal-held notes remain mechanically distinguishable throughout the dense fixture.
- Near-white coverage is capped except for a brief arrival; compare luminance histograms, but never use them as an eye verdict.
- A safe-area overlay proves primary text and meaningful key action survive typical platform chrome.
- Same MIDI log + preset + seed yields the same arrival timestamps and visual events.

**By eye, on all six evidence frames and new candidates:**
1. **One-second glance:** can I identify finger-held versus pedal-held and the current musical thought?
2. **Squint:** is there one focal event or an evenly bright wall?
3. **Mute:** can a stranger see quiet, build, arrival and release?
4. **Sound-only prediction:** did emphasis land where the phrase earned it?
5. **Daniel:** does he keep playing, laugh, point at a moment or ask to see it again? If he starts managing the visualizer instead of forgetting it, it failed as an instrument.

Have Daniel, one musician and one stranger choose the strongest frame before anyone explains the features. Measurement catches broken presentation; the eye still decides beauty.
