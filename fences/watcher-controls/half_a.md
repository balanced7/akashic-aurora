## Notes on the mechanism-vs-analogy line (the iteration, condensed)

Two rejections, argued on the bus and reproduced here verbatim-in-spirit so the reconciliation sees them:

1. **SIS voting and de-energize-to-trip do not apply (V3).** Voting assumes N independent sensors measuring one quantity. Our two worklive keys are one quantity written into two namespaces by three harnesses writing different subsets (liveness.py:258-259 names the split; roster.py:349-353 reads it). There is no N in {1,2} that is correct. De-energize-to-trip is field-device physics we do not have. The correct frame is quality codes (V6). On-bus I overclaimed once ("both keys written by the same heartbeat thread") and corrected it: the bare key is written by `WorkLive._flush` (heartbeat) and the #key by the runner/runtime roster beat — two writers, two projections of one fact, still not two sensors.

2. **Anti-windup does not apply to the breaker flood (V9).** Windup is a saturated integrator overshooting later; the 22-page flood has no integrator, it is a level-triggered emission over a condition with no owner to clear it (the brief's own evidence note). The real name for "re-broadcast without a state change" is chatter, and chatter is the alarm state machine's problem (V1), not anti-windup's. Graded UNCERTAIN/teaches-false as named.

A third correction I flag without a full rejection: the lens's first-scan bit as the model for "arm-time level check" is subtly wrong. The first-scan bit is a ONE-SCAN global non-retentive edge; our arm-onto-pending contract is a PER-ARM level read over durable obligation state — that is the OPPOSITE of an edge, it is a level read. R_TRIG (edge-from-level) is the real analog, and it maps to the consume handoff, not the arm. I folded first-scan out of V4 rather than let it teach "arm-time evaluation is an edge."
